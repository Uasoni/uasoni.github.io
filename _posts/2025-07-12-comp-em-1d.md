## Derivation of Update Equations

Expanding the two curl equations:

<div>
$$
\frac{\partial E_z}{\partial y}
- \frac{\partial E_y}{\partial z}
= -\frac{1}{c_{0}}
\left(
  \mu_{xx}\frac{\partial \tilde H_x}{\partial t}
+ \mu_{xy}\frac{\partial \tilde H_y}{\partial t}
+ \mu_{xz}\frac{\partial \tilde H_z}{\partial t}
\right)
$$
</div>


<div>
$$
\frac{\partial E_x}{\partial z}
- \frac{\partial E_z}{\partial x}
= -\frac{1}{c_{0}}
\left(
  \mu_{yx}\frac{\partial \tilde H_x}{\partial t}
+ \mu_{yy}\frac{\partial \tilde H_y}{\partial t}
+ \mu_{yz}\frac{\partial \tilde H_z}{\partial t}
\right)
$$
</div>


<div>
$$
\frac{\partial E_y}{\partial x}
- \frac{\partial E_x}{\partial y}
= -\frac{1}{c_{0}}
\left(
  \mu_{zx}\frac{\partial \tilde H_x}{\partial t}
+ \mu_{zy}\frac{\partial \tilde H_y}{\partial t}
+ \mu_{zz}\frac{\partial \tilde H_z}{\partial t}
\right)
$$
</div>


<div>
$$
\frac{\partial \tilde H_z}{\partial y}
- \frac{\partial \tilde H_y}{\partial z}
= \frac{1}{c_{0}}
\left(
  \varepsilon_{xx}\frac{\partial E_x}{\partial t}
+ \varepsilon_{xy}\frac{\partial E_y}{\partial t}
+ \varepsilon_{xz}\frac{\partial E_z}{\partial t}
\right)
$$
</div>


<div>
$$
\frac{\partial \tilde H_x}{\partial z}
- \frac{\partial \tilde H_z}{\partial x}
= \frac{1}{c_{0}}
\left(
  \varepsilon_{yx}\frac{\partial E_x}{\partial t}
+ \varepsilon_{yy}\frac{\partial E_y}{\partial t}
+ \varepsilon_{yz}\frac{\partial E_z}{\partial t}
\right)
$$
</div>


<div>
$$
\frac{\partial \tilde H_y}{\partial x}
- \frac{\partial \tilde H_x}{\partial y}
= \frac{1}{c_{0}}
\left(
  \varepsilon_{zx}\frac{\partial E_x}{\partial t}
+ \varepsilon_{zy}\frac{\partial E_y}{\partial t}
+ \varepsilon_{zz}\frac{\partial E_z}{\partial t}
\right)
$$
</div>

We assume "diagonally anisotropic" materials (and later isotropic materials) - i.e. working in crystal axes of the permittivity and permeability tensors - so off-diagonal entries in the permittivity/permeability vanish:


<div>
$$
\frac{\partial E_z}{\partial y}
- \frac{\partial E_y}{\partial z}
= -\frac{\mu_{xx}}{c_{0}} \frac{\partial \tilde H_x}{\partial t}
$$
</div>


<div>
$$
\frac{\partial E_x}{\partial z}
- \frac{\partial E_z}{\partial x}
= -\frac{\mu_{yy}}{c_{0}} \frac{\partial \tilde H_y}{\partial t}
$$
</div>


<div>
$$
\frac{\partial E_y}{\partial x}
- \frac{\partial E_x}{\partial y}
= -\frac{\mu_{zz}}{c_{0}} \frac{\partial \tilde H_z}{\partial t}
$$
</div>


<div>
$$
\frac{\partial \tilde H_z}{\partial y}
- \frac{\partial \tilde H_y}{\partial z}
= \frac{\varepsilon_{xx}}{c_{0}} \frac{\partial E_x}{\partial t}
$$
</div>


<div>
$$
\frac{\partial \tilde H_x}{\partial z}
- \frac{\partial \tilde H_z}{\partial x}
= \frac{\varepsilon_{yy}}{c_{0}} \frac{\partial E_y}{\partial t}
$$
</div>


<div>
$$
\frac{\partial \tilde H_y}{\partial x}
- \frac{\partial \tilde H_x}{\partial y}
= \frac{\varepsilon_{zz}}{c_{0}} \frac{\partial E_z}{\partial t}
$$
</div>

Since we are working 1D, we set <span class="inline-math">$\frac{ \partial }{ \partial y }=\frac{ \partial }{ \partial z }=0$</span>, and have the simulation run on the x-axis. With this, the curl equations simplify into (leaving out the ones of the form <span class="inline-math">$0=\text{RHS}$</span>):

<div>
$$
\frac{\partial E_z}{\partial x}
= \frac{\mu_{yy}}{c_{0}} \frac{\partial \tilde H_y}{\partial t}
$$
</div>


<div>
$$
\frac{\partial E_y}{\partial x}
= -\frac{\mu_{zz}}{c_{0}} \frac{\partial \tilde H_z}{\partial t}
$$
</div>


<div>
$$
\frac{\partial \tilde H_z}{\partial x}
= - \frac{\varepsilon_{yy}}{c_{0}} \frac{\partial E_y}{\partial t}
$$
</div>


<div>
$$
\frac{\partial \tilde H_y}{\partial x}
= \frac{\varepsilon_{zz}}{c_{0}} \frac{\partial E_z}{\partial t}
$$
</div>

The first and last interact, while the 2nd and 3rd interact. This is the decoupling into the <span class="inline-math">$E_z$</span>-<span class="inline-math">$H_y$</span> mode and the <span class="inline-math">$E_y$</span>-<span class="inline-math">$H_z$</span> mode mentioned previously in setup.

To simplify things further, for isotropy (<span class="inline-math">$\mu_{yy}=\mu_{zz},\, \varepsilon_{yy}=\varepsilon_{zz}$</span>), the two equations describe the same thing (by inspection). Hence only necessary to solve one (<span class="inline-math">$E_z$</span>-<span class="inline-math">$H_y$</span>).

Following the same discretisation, we have

<div>
$$\begin{align}\frac{E_z^{i+1}(t)-E_z^i(t)}{dx} &= \frac{\mu^i}{c_{0}} \frac{\tilde H^i_y\left( t+\frac{dt}{2} \right)-\tilde H^i_y\left( t-\frac{dt}{2} \right)}{dt} \\ \implies \tilde H_y^i\left( t+\frac{dt}{2} \right) &= \boxed{\tilde H_y^i\left( t-\frac{dt}{2} \right)+ \frac{c_0\,dt}{\mu^i}\left(\frac{E_z^{i+1}(t)-E_z^i(t)}{dx}\right)} \end{align}$$
</div>

for the magnetic field at position <span class="inline-math">$i$</span>, and

<div>
$$\begin{align}\frac{\tilde H_y^i\left( t+\frac{dt}{2} \right) - \tilde H_y^{i-1}\left( t+\frac{dt}{2} \right)}{dx} &= \frac{\varepsilon^i}{c_0}\frac{E_z^i(t+dt)-E_z^i(t)}{dt} \\ \implies E_z^i(t+dt) &= \boxed{E_z^i(t)+\frac{c_0\, dt}{\varepsilon^i} \left(\frac{\tilde H_y^i\left( t+\frac{dt}{2} \right) - \tilde H_y^{i-1}\left( t+\frac{dt}{2} \right)}{dx}\right)}\end{align}$$
</div>

These are the **update equations**.

## Implementation

### Free Space

```python
import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
import scipy.integrate as spi
import matplotlib.animation as animation

N = 500  # Number of grid points
dx = 0.01  # Spatial step
dt = 0.01  # Time step
T = 50.0  # Total time
c0 = 1.0  # Speed of light

H = np.zeros(N)  # Magnetic field (y)
E = np.zeros(N)  # Electric field (z)

eps = 1.0  # Permittivity
mu = 5  # Permeability
h_coeff = c0*dt/(mu*dx)  # Update coefficient for H
e_coeff = c0*dt/(eps*dx)  # Update coefficient for E

def update(half_step):
    global H, E
    if half_step:
        for i in range(0, N-1):
            H[i] += h_coeff * (E[i+1] - E[i])
    else:
        for i in range(1, N):
            E[i] += e_coeff * (H[i] - H[i-1])

def run_simulation(steps):
    global H, E
    hist_H = np.zeros((steps, N))
    hist_E = np.zeros((steps, N))
    for step in range(steps):
        update(1)
        hist_H[step] = H.copy()
        update(0)
        hist_E[step] = E.copy()

    fig, ax = plt.subplots()
    line_H, = ax.plot(np.arange(N) * dx, hist_H[0], label='H(y)')
    line_E, = ax.plot(np.arange(N) * dx, hist_E[0], label='E(z)')
    ax.set_xlabel('Position (x)')
    ax.set_ylabel('Field Value')
    ax.set_title('1D FDTD Simulation')
    ax.set_xlim(0, N * dx)
    ax.set_ylim(-1, 1)
    ax.grid()
    ax.legend()

    time_text = ax.text(0.02, 0.95, '', transform=ax.transAxes)

    def animate(i):
        line_H.set_ydata(hist_H[i])
        line_E.set_ydata(hist_E[i])
        time_text.set_text(f't={i*dt:.2f}s')
        return [line_H, line_E, time_text]
    anim = animation.FuncAnimation(fig, animate, frames=steps, interval=10, blit=True)

    plt.show()

def create_gaussian_pulse(c, r, A=1.0):
    t = np.linspace(0, N*dx, N)
    return A * np.exp(-((t-c)**2)/(2*r**2))

E = create_gaussian_pulse(N*dx/2, 0.1, 1.0)

run_simulation(int(T//dt))
```

Most of this is as described exactly in the theory. Note that the coefficients literally don't matter so I set <span class="inline-math">$c_0=1$</span>.

Furthermore, stability analysis shows that it isn't enough to just perform a 'hard source' injection by setting `E[N//2] = 1.0`, since the large gradient between adjacent grid points causes numeric instability. Hence, I wrote a Gaussian pulse to be instantiated at <span class="inline-math">$t=0$</span> as a 'hard source'.

Seems relatively numerically stable, and also behaves as expected with Dirichlet boundary conditions. Turned the permeability to <span class="inline-math">$\mu=5$</span> so that the H field looks different to the E field for testing purposes.

### Materials Testing

Now turning `eps` and `mu` into arrays, as well as the update coefficients in order to test behaviour within different materials (and boundary conditions along those materials).

```python
import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
import scipy.integrate as spi
import matplotlib.animation as animation

N = 500  # Number of grid points
dx = 0.01  # Spatial step
dt = 0.01  # Time step
T = 10.0  # Total time
c0 = 1.0  # Speed of light

H = np.zeros(N)  # Magnetic field (y)
E = np.zeros(N)  # Electric field (z)

def create_gaussian_pulse(c, r, A=1.0):
    t = np.linspace(0, N*dx, N)
    return A * np.exp(-((t-c)**2)/(2*r**2))
E = create_gaussian_pulse(N*dx/2, 0.1, 1.0)

eps = np.ones(N)  # Permittivity
mu = np.ones(N)  # Permeability

def create_material(start, end, permittivity=1.0, permeability=1.0):
    global eps, mu
    start_idx = int(start / dx)
    end_idx = int(end / dx)
    eps[start_idx:end_idx] = permittivity
    mu[start_idx:end_idx] = permeability
MATERIALS = [
    (3.5, 4.5, 1.0, 5.0)
]
for i in MATERIALS:
    create_material(*i)

h_coeff = c0*dt/(mu*dx)  # Update coefficients for H
e_coeff = c0*dt/(eps*dx)  # Update coefficients for E

def update(half_step):
    global H, E
    if half_step:
        for i in range(0, N-1):
            H[i] += h_coeff[i] * (E[i+1] - E[i])
    else:
        for i in range(1, N):
            E[i] += e_coeff[i] * (H[i] - H[i-1])

def run_simulation(steps):
    global H, E, MATERIALS
    hist_H = np.zeros((steps, N))
    hist_E = np.zeros((steps, N))
    for step in range(steps):
        update(1)
        hist_H[step] = H.copy()
        update(0)
        hist_E[step] = E.copy()

    fig, ax = plt.subplots()

    for i in MATERIALS:
        ax.axvspan(i[0], i[1], color=((i[2]-1)/9, 0, (i[3]-1)/9, 0.5))

    line_H, = ax.plot(np.arange(N) * dx, hist_H[0], label='H(y)')
    line_E, = ax.plot(np.arange(N) * dx, hist_E[0], label='E(z)')
    ax.set_xlabel('Position (x)')
    ax.set_ylabel('Field Value')
    ax.set_title('1D FDTD Simulation')
    ax.set_xlim(0, N * dx)
    ax.set_ylim(-1, 1)
    ax.grid()
    ax.legend()

    time_text = ax.text(0.02, 0.95, '', transform=ax.transAxes)

    def animate(i):
        line_H.set_ydata(hist_H[i])
        line_E.set_ydata(hist_E[i])
        time_text.set_text(f't={i*dt:.2f}s')
        return [line_H, line_E, time_text]
    anim = animation.FuncAnimation(fig, animate, frames=steps, interval=10, blit=True)
    
    plt.show()

run_simulation(int(T//dt))
```

Added a way of adding regions of materials with particular <span class="inline-math">$\varepsilon$</span> and <span class="inline-math">$\mu$</span> values, as well as a visualiser for the materials in the form of shaded regions, where more red means higher permittivity, and more blue means higher permeability.

>N.B. I assume that <span class="inline-math">$\max(\max_i(\varepsilon),\max_i(\mu))=10$</span> for now.

### Issues

There is a 'quirk' with the simulation, being that as soon as <span class="inline-math">$dx$</span> is *low enough* to force the update coefficients to exceed 1, the simulation explodes and numerically diverges extremely quickly. Not exactly sure why but there's no problem if you don't see it right team?

This fixes itself when:
- <span class="inline-math">$dx$</span> is increased, or
- <span class="inline-math">$dt$</span> is decreased, or
- <span class="inline-math">$\varepsilon$</span> and <span class="inline-math">$\mu$</span> are decreased to compensate
It's highly unintuitive that a smaller <span class="inline-math">$dx$</span> somehow causes it to be *less* numerically stable... but I think the fundamental problem lies in what happens when the update coefficient exceeds 1.

What's stranger is one update coefficient can be less than 1 while another is greater than 1, and it might still be stable (if the one that's greater is able to 'compensate' somehow).
