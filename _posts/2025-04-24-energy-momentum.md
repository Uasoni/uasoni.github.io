## Expressions

In relativistic dynamics,

<div>
$$\mathbf p=\gamma_v m\mathbf v,\, E=\gamma mc^2$$
</div>

where <span class="inline-math">$m$</span> is the rest mass. These quantities are conserved in *all* collisions. These have been empirically proven to be true. A rationalisation can be made through 4-vectors.

This also implies a relationship between momentum, energy, and velocity -

<div>
$$\frac{\mathbf{p}}{E}=\frac{\mathbf{v}}{c^2}$$
</div>

implying any two gives us the other.

Furthermore, for collisions,

<div>
$$E^2-|\mathbf p|^2c^2=\gamma^2m^2c^4\left( 1-\frac{v^2}{c^2} \right)=(mc^2)^2$$
</div>

usually expressed as <span class="inline-math">$E^2=p^2c^2+m^2c^4$</span>. Notably for photons (<span class="inline-math">$m=0$</span>) this reduces to <span class="inline-math">$E=pc$</span>.

>When <span class="inline-math">$v\ll c$</span> and <span class="inline-math">$\gamma \to 1$</span>, we have that
>
<div>
$$\mathbf p \approx m\mathbf v, \, E\approx mc^2+\frac{1}{2}mv^2+\cdots$$
</div>

>where we neglect higher-order terms in <span class="inline-math">$\frac{v}{c}$</span> for energy. These match Newtonian expectation; for energy when the collision is elastic, there is no change to mass - hence
>
<div>
$$E_1=E_2\implies \frac{1}{2}mv_1^2=\frac{1}{2}mv_2^2$$
</div>

>For non elastic collisions, heat is converted into a small amount of mass. Hence <span class="inline-math">$mc^2$</span> terms on each side differ, and conservation of 'kinetic' energy (the <span class="inline-math">$\frac{1}{2}mv^2$</span> term) does not hold.
